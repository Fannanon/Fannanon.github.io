The First Test Ride: Mundaring Weir and Back

Monday, 9th November 2015

You can spend all night reading CGOAB journals, trawling through pages and pages of contradicting advice on the bike forums and Reddit, but at the end of the day nothing exposes flaws in a plan better than a good old fashioned real world test. To that end, the past few months have seen me take off on a few rides and overnighters to try and gain a bit of experience riding fully loaded and then setting up a cosy camp for the night.

The first ride was a simple loaded ride into the Perth hills, no camping involved.

I loaded Shirley up with front and rear panniers containing some random clothes, camping equipment, kitchen equipment, some snacks and a bit of spare water. As I took off down my street, I was pretty damn happy with the way the bike felt while carrying a load. This is what you pay good money for when you fork out for a proper touring bike!

My ride took me down the highway to the foot of the Darling Ranges where I faced my first major hill. I picked a good one.. About 300m of elevation gained in just under 2km with a maximum gradient of approximately 14%. Needless to say, I was in the lowest of the very low gears available on the bike. My heart pounded in my chest and I'm fairly sure the sound of me gasping for air disturbed a lady out walking her dog - she definitely gave me a weird look. Happily, I made it to the top of that bastard without getting off the bike, but I definitely took time for a breather and some fruit once I made it into the town of Kalamunda.

From there it was a case of riding the undulating road out of town and down to Mundaring Weir. There I ran into the weekend warrior road cyclists of Perth and I have to say, almost every last one of them gave me some form of encouragement as they rode past, from a simple wave to a tale of the time they went on tour back in the day! Very nice! I soon found a decent rhythm to my pedalling and settled into a steady pace. Eventually, I reached the dam and the half way point of my ride.

My homeward journey had a couple of notable moments. The first was a clipless pedal incident. A guy in his four wheel drive either didn't see me or didn't care to see me and cut me off as he pulled into a driveway intersecting my path. I didn't have much time to react and as I furiously tried to unclip my feet from my pedals, I managed to unbalance my heavily loaded bike. I went down in the classic, awkward, failure to unclip style. Embarrassing stuff but a good bit of exposure to the hazards of a heavy bike!

The second incident highlighted a flaw in my packing system. As I crossed a railway intersection, a nice guy in a nearby car yelled out to let me know that I'd just lost some gear! I looked back and saw my sleeping bag and mat lying in the road. With a wave to the nice fellow in the car and a few muttered curses, I jogged over and quickly scooped up my wayward belongings before the traffic lights changed. Clearly my plan to tie a large portion of my gear down to the rear rack would need some reassessment.

In the end I made it home in one piece. Strava reported a 75km ride with just over 1000m of elevation gain. I was knackered, but feeling reasonably optimistic...
