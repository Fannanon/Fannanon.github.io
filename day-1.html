Day 1 – San Francisco to Winters

Sunday, 3rd April 2016

The first day of my tour started with a fairly rubbish sleep. While in San Francisco I stayed in one of the cheaper Air B&B options available. I had a new roommate every night I was there! On two occasions, the new roommate appeared in the middle of the night! In last night's case, my roomie was in town for a half marathon. We chatted about carbohydrates and how important a good rest is for endurance activities. I thought that given that he was fit enough to run a marathon, he'd be a silent sleeper. Wrong!! A snorer and a sleep talker too! Ah well, that's what I get for being cheap!

I awoke pretty pumped and ready to go despite the above complaints! With the bike loaded, I closed the garage on the Air B&B and ambled down 33rd Avenue. I was actually supposed to amble up the avenue and had to turn around. My first wrong turn was out of the way at least.

The ride through San Francisco was lovely. I don't think any of the rides I took in the city could be described otherwise. I rode the coastal trail, through the park, past the bridge and down to the shore where I snapped a photo of the bike by the ocean. Some riders dip a tyre in the water per tradition. Salt water and sand do not mix well with a greasy chain and knowing me, any attempt to drag a heavy bike across the beach would end in disaster so I had to pass on that one.

I dodged and weaved my way through the hordes of tourists at Pier 39 and wondered again what the attraction to that part of town is? It seems to do a great job of keeping the more obnoxious tourists away from the good parts of town though, so hats off to whoever came up with that idea.

The ferry ride out to Vallejo was sad in a way. I was in no way done with San Francisco. It seems I'll need to learn how to code and become a tech bro to make the move there though. It's far too expensive for anyone not pulling in the big bucks, and that's coming from someone from Perth! I can see why there is a lot of tension surrounding the affordability issue. If you'd spent your whole life living somewhere as beautiful as San Francisco and found yourself being priced out, it would have to be pretty galling. The city still had enough magic to quickly cast a spell this foreigner though, I hope it never loses that quality.

As I came ashore in Vallejo, I said farewell to the other guy with a bike on the ferry. I almost feel ashamed when I tell people where I'm headed. I'm so far from the destination and there is so much hard riding ahead. I might start believing when I get over Carson Pass in a week or so.

The ride through and out of Vallejo was surprisingly hilly! There were a few good little climbs on the Columbus Parkway and the Solano bike path that gave a great view of the massive interstate highway and the Six Flags off in the distance. Everything in the U.S seems huge to me, from the highways to the hills. Hopefully this feeling of being overawed sticks around, the miles feel like inches when every corner presents a new great sight.

I hit wine country and rode some gorgeous roads through the vines. At the back of each estate was a stately manor, star spangled banner in front of everyone. It's certainly a kick seeing all this Americana that movies and TV have exposed me to over the years! The route took me through the housing estates of Fairfield, perfect in every way it seemed from the fresh bitumen on the road to the impeccably manicured gardens. Maybe a little too perfect if I'm being honest. Pleasants Valley road made up much of the rest of the ride. That's pleasant with an S. It was extremely pleasant and I suppose you could say there were a few different pleasantries to be had, but the name still bugs me somehow.

My target for the day was Davis and a host who had kindly offered a couch to crash on, but the sun was getting low in the sky and my belly rumbled so I opted for a campsite near the town of Winters. The park rangers directed me to a bikers and hikers site, but upon checking the prices (25 bucks), I turned around and thought to head for Davis. As I was about to leave the park rangers pulled alongside and said;

"Don't you like our park?!'

I explained that I thought it a bit pricey. They kindly informed me that a special rate applies for bikers and hikers, a mere 5 bucks compared to the 25 for your more traditional travellers. We had a good chat about the route I was taking and about my adventures so far. Real nice guys who seemed generally concerned with the welfare of lunatics on bikes!

I set up camp and jumped on my now much lighter bike for the quick 6k ride into Winters where I absolutely destroyed a Hawaiian pizza. Upon returning to camp, the computer reported 106km ridden today. It sure feels like it. Time for some well-earned rest, I think. Tomorrow, I get to see my uncle Alan and his family in El Dorado hills! Woohoo!
