Day 2 – Winters to El Dorado Hills

Monday, 4th April 2016

Day two started with another rubbish sleep!

This time it was the local peacocks! I'm so used to pitch blackness and perfect silence when I sleep, so this sort of thing is an unforeseen challenge. Jet lag is still with me somehow too. The time shift from Perth to the West Coast of the U.S is near as dramatic as it gets. My body is still convinced that I should be up and about at 4am local time!

I made a nice warm oats and coffee breakfast on my shiny new propane stove, the MSR Pocketrocket. Between that and my new set of pots into which the stove fits neatly, it's a great lightweight solution for making a tasty breakfast.

I hit the tarmac at about 9am in the end which is a bit later than I hope to set off from here on out. It just doesn't leave enough time to stop and see the sights! Putah Creek road took me most of the way through to the town of Davis, flat, fast riding through orange and olive plantations on one side and the creek on the other. The California sun shone through the branches making for quite the idyllic scene!

I scooted into the bicycling capital of the U.S around 10:30. The two wheeled traffic increased as I approached UC Davis and quickly became a torrent upon crossing the boundary into the campus. The many bike paths that run through the facility intersect at roundabouts where helmetless students whizz around with reckless abandon (they don't even indicate, oh my word!). As a bit of a cycling infrastructure nerd, it was like something out of a dream. People out in the sunshine getting from A to B on a bike without overthinking it. You just get on your bike and ride!

I was way overdressed in my cleated MTB shoes and bike jersey. My shiny touring bike with multiple panniers and handlebar bag was quite out of place among the thousands of beater bikes locked up to the racks. A lot of the disparaging remarks about cyclists back home in Perth revolve around the choice of attire of local cyclists (Lycra) and the perception that it's a pastime for yuppies with too much money to spend on carbon fibre toys. I'm convinced that good infrastructure can normalise bike riding. A trip to the shop for a litre of milk shouldn't require a car or padded cycling shorts. Davis has a near 50 year head start on this, they figured it out back in the 60s. I hope my home can move just a little more in that direction and just a little faster. We've got traffic problems. A bit of investment in anything other than more lanes on the freeway might be a good start.

I downed a college priced veggie burrito for lunch, a mere 5 bucks! It was large and delicious, if a little heavy for a days cycling. I made a quick exit from town and made for Sacramento via a combination of a truck route with a generous shoulder and the Yolo (lol) Causeway bike path. A highway patrol car had pulled someone over westbound near the beginning of the causeway and as I proceeded to the east, the traffic steadily banked up into a full-on traffic jam. Damn rubberneckers! I sure was glad to be on the deserted bike path heading the opposite way with a light wind at my back. I got down into the drops of my handlebars and maintained a steady 30kph the whole way which ain't half bad for a stupidly heavy touring bike if I may say so myself!

I entered Sacramento through an industrial estate and crossed into the city itself via Tower Bridge, a draw bridge much like its bigger brother in London. I swung a left into Old Town, and found myself riding on a boardwalk. The bike did not love the surface and transmitted every bump into my bones. I looked for a way off and found myself on some cobblestones instead. Damn you historic road surface!

I can't really say I've been to Sacramento to be honest. The American River bike trail runs through a huge greenway that cuts the city in two. Beyond the numerous rail and freeway overpasses, you'd barely realise that you're in the middle of a metropolis of over 400,000 people! There is an abundance of wildlife to be spotted in the wetland. Apparently Australia is home to all the world scary animals but I saw two snakes just chilling on the path. That's more snakes than I've seen in a lifetime in Aus. The first one nearly went under my wheel as I was too busy staring at the scenery to notice him! When he popped into the corner of my eye, the monkey man in me experienced that evolutionary reaction most of us have when we see the dangerous animal. A surge of adrenalin and a swerve of the bike and both snake and I made it through the encounter in one piece.

I emerged from the bike trail into Folsom. I should have taken on more food in the afternoon as I could feel the dreaded 'bonk' (no carbs left to burn) coming on as I passed the 100km mark riding through town. The lack of a decent sleep probably didn't help either. I still had a fair way to go to make it to my uncles house in El Dorado Hills and my legs were feeling a bit weak. I decided I'd try and take a shortcut through the suburbs to cut off a few miles. I popped my uncles address into Google maps and set it for a cycling route. It made me take a bike path through the back of the suburbs until I reached the foot of a giant hill. Here it told me to take a u-turn and go back the way I came. It turns out I had entered 852 instead of 825 for my uncles address. Rather than simply telling me the address didn't exist, Google sent me on a bullshit route to nowhere. Sigh. At this point I was well off my Adventure Cycling America approved route and had no choice but to rely on my tech again. I entered the correct address and this time a route popped up that made sense.

The giant hill I mentioned earlier happened to be the same hill upon which my uncles house was situated. The incline on the roads leading there were as steep as any I've ever encountered and totally not what a guy in the middle of a carbohydrate breakdown needs. So close yet so far. I put the bike into the lowest gear and proceeded up the hill at walking pace. As I could feel the goal within reach, my body provided me with one last burst of energy and I powered up the hill onto my uncles street. He opened the door and gave me a hug with a slap on my gross sweaty back.

I'll be staying with him for a few days to chill and see a bit more of California before I leave the state. I've resolved to ditch some of my load which I'm sure isn't necessary. I'm hoping to finally iron out the kinks in my circadian rhythms too! The Western Express gives you little time to learn. The next two days of riding will be some of the hardest of the trip. Eep.
