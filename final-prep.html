Final Prep

Friday, 5th February 2016

One more test run was in order. To be honest, I would have liked to have had the opportunity to do a longer tour to get a proper feel for riding day after day and camping in between. The Aussie summer and the need to save what little annual leave I had with my employer put an end to that ambition. So, for my last little trip I stayed local again, aiming for Lake Leschenaultia, a lake formed by a dam out in the Perth hills. Dee would drive to meet me there so we could enjoy a night of camping together before we split up for the duration of my tour.

Most of my packing issues had been ironed out during the previous test runs and as such, the ride was pretty smooth. I had extended my daily work commute and had ventured out for some long weekend rides since my trip down to Dwellingup so I was certainly feeling a bit stronger on the bike this time round. I mixed some big hills into the route including my favourite, Crystal Brook Road. A short but steep ascent with a nice kicker at the end. There is nothing you can do but slip into the lowest gear and spin I guess. Make up lost time on the descents. Dream of a road bike.

The day had started warm at sunrise and only got hotter from there. The classic Perth 'dry heat,' a kind of heat that cooks the road and evaporates sweat so fast you could be forgiven for thinking you're not breaking one. I'm pretty green when it comes to dealing with the cold, but a hot day is definitely something that living in Perth has given me some experience in. I dealt with this days heat by checking into a local servo (gas station) for some Gatorade and enjoyed just a little bit of air con. Sue me, the sun was angry that day.

The last 20 or so k's of riding went by fast and I pulled up to the lake ahead of schedule. I had a quick swim in the meantime. I guess Lake Leschenaultia is kind of like the beach for the locals of the Perth hills. It's fully equipped with barbecues, tables, lots of shade and a sandy shore to plop a towel on and absorb some Vitamin D and UV rays rated as 'Extreme' by the Bureau of Meteorology. The place was packed with families enjoying the heat. I joined them in the cool water at which point my leg muscles immediately cramped, forcing me to doggie paddle back to shore. I am no triathlete.

Dee arrived with a beautiful packed lunch, a luxury I don't expect on Highway 50, Nevada. Following another swim, we observed a large group of girls who had split into teams and set themselves the challenge of building rafts made of household items. We watched the spectacle from afar, critiquing each group's raft building skills and team communication. Naturally, there were many things I thought they could be doing a bit better but I thought it best not to taint their experience with my advanced knowledge and elected to stay out of it. A great man, Captain Jean Luc Picard, taught me the utmost importance of the Prime Directive.

As the sun set, the hot easterly that had blown all day faded and the lake surface transformed into a mirror for the red sky. I had planned to show off my bush cooking skills to Dee by making a veggie curry for dinner on the Trangia stove however a total fire ban was in place and the park ranger would not allow it! Coincidentally, a massive blaze had torn through a huge area just south of my Dwellingup campsite in the weeks after my stay there, so it was definitely advice to take seriously. We opted for the local pizza instead. On the way to get it my old car started making a clunking sound. Hmmm...

The next morning we made cinnamon oats with powdered milk and caramelised apple on the barbecue by placing the Trangia pots directly onto the hot plate. A decent workaround for the fire ban. Sadly, given the clunking sounds in the car I had to forgo my ride home to ensure Dee wouldn't find herself stranded alone if the car broke down. Sure enough the car broke down. A tow truck was required and 350 bucks later we were home. God I hate that car.

In the weeks that followed I trained hard to try and shape up. Regular commuting on my touring bike served to break in the leather Brooks saddle some more and keep me at a good base fitness. Long weekend rides in the hills were thrown in and I completed the Kalamunda 100 event which involved some 2000m of climbing! At the time of writing this, my legs are feeling good but sadly my arms are now next to useless. I may consider doing a push-up or two in the near future.

Going into the tour I feel like fitness is the least of my worries. I know that given a day to ride I can put in some decent miles. The tests I fear most will be the mental ones. On a cold lonely road, will I feel like giving in? Will I miss Dee, my family and my friends too much? I wanted out of my comfort zone when I decided this tour was an idea worth pursuing and I'm sure it will put me well outside the boundaries. It's what I want and it's what I'll get!

I'm writing this journal entry 4 hours out from San Francisco, sandwiched between two sleeping giants and one row behind a family with a kid who communicates only in screams, yet my spirits are up and I'm feeling excited to hit the ground running. Next stop, the bike assembly station at San Francisco International. I do hope Shirley had a nice flight..
