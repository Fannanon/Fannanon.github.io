A Week in El Dorado Hills

So I've spent the last week in El Dorado Hills enjoying the company of my uncle Alan, his wife Susan and their daughter Skylar. I've had a very relaxing time, maybe a little too relaxing if I'm being completely honest! I can safely say that I'm 100% over my jet lag, so no longer will that be wheeled out as an excuse for sub par performances on the bike. I couldn't ask for warmer hosts, it's been an absolute pleasure staying with them!

Living the suburban Californian life has been an experience in itself. We went down the road to a British/Irish themed pub for some fish and chips on my first night here. I can't say they quite captured the essence of a British pub, but then it really is kind of impossible to do that without the ancient history that comes with a lot of the great old establishments back in Blighty. Nothing wrong with the meal and beer though, a perfect reward for a hard day's ride.

The second night we went to an Australian themed steakhouse called Outback. We called ahead for a booking and the recorded message started with 'g'day mate'. Ooooh boy. The restaurant itself was hilarious. Wooden crocodiles on the shelves and meals that came with the 'Special Aussie' mac and cheese side. There was a roundabout sign on the wall too. Seriously America, you should have more of these on the road and less in your themed restaurants. Four way stop signs are just nuts. I had a great meal here though and discovered the greatness of cheesy fries.

I kept myself busy with a few other adventures over the course of the week. I visited Placerville and stopped by the oldest hardware store in California for some cable ties. The owner was a font of knowledge, informing me that the floorboards upon which I walked were the originals from hundreds of years ago. Kinda cool to imagine some of the bad ass old west folks who must have visited the store all those years ago. I'm fretting about crossing Nevada on a bike in 2016, but I can't imagine the life cattle drivers would have lead back in the day. I also saw the Folsom Prison Museum which among many grim artefacts, also contained Rick James' guitar from his time spent behind bars there. Cool.

One thing I enjoy doing whenever I can, is reading local news. The more parochial the better. Alan had left a copy of El Dorado county's local rag on the kitchen table and it was in here that I read about some beavers that had been causing the locals of nearby Serrano some grief. Alan hates the Serrano folks. It's a brand new housing estate on the other side of the main road and by all accounts, the people there are a different breed who live above and somewhat apart from the rest of El Dorado Hills. I'm inclined to believe it after what I read and saw.

A green belt runs through the middle of one of the gated communities of Serrano. In the middle is a drainage canal alongside which some nice trees and shrubs have sprouted. Local houses have been built on the greenway and of late, the residents have been none too happy with the logging activity of the local beaver population. The canal is purportedly backing up and turning into a stagnant swamp. The home owners association is 'powerless' to do anything. Naturally, I felt inclined to investigate the situation. It was a good excuse to keep my legs fresh with a ride up Serrano Parkway, a decent sized hill leading to the housing estate. I thought it might be cool to see a beaver's lodge too and maybe even a beaver!

Getting into the gated community wasn't exactly difficult, I simply tailed an SUV in. I wonder if the residents ever consider how easy it is to get through this advanced security measure? The houses and gardens inside were immaculate to the point that they appeared to be made out of plastic. There wasn't a soul in sight or earshot. Just wide, empty streets lined with cookie cutter houses! Gated communities exist back in Australia but I can't say I've ever seen anything quite like this.

I rode the streets, past more than a few Ted Cruz 2016 signs (heh, good luck) until I found the greenway referred to in the newspaper. With nobody around to stop me, I left the path against the urging of the nearby signs and went down to the canal. Here I saw the 'stagnant pool' A four square meter pond had developed behind a beaver lodge. The water still flowed mind you, just a little slower. I saw the signs of beavers gnawing at the nearby trees but sadly, didn't sight one of the animals. Turns out they're kinda nocturnal. Oh well!

The whole experience was worth it, if only to catch a glimpse at this strange slice of American suburbia. The Serrano estate has been plonked on top of a hill and is surrounded by nature. It's actually a gorgeous piece of land and I have to say, the locals getting wound up by the activity of a few beavers who've probably been there a fair bit longer than them is pretty damn funny. You can't have a nature strip an then try to exert 100% control over the nature in the strip, people. Satisfied I'd seen enough, I pedalled my judgemental Australian ass out of there and flew back down the parkway back into the real world.

The remainder of my time in El Dorado Hills was spent waiting for a significant rain storm to pass. After endless California sunshine, it had to happen eventually. Unfortunately it happened right around the time I had planned to leave! Thankfully the weather from here on out is looking relatively good and I'm definitely ready to get into the meat of this tour, that being the Sierra Nevada and the desert on the other side. Tally ho!
