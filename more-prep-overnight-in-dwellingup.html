More Prep – Overnight in Dwellingup

Saturday, 19th December 2015

The next phase of preparation for this tour was an overnighter just outside of the town of Dwellingup, about 120km ride south of Perth.

The route I followed was largely on a shared path that runs alongside the Kwinana Freeway, Perth's main southbound route out of town. It's a pretty awesome piece of bike infrastructure, no doubt about it! Over 100km of the finest bitumen squeezed in between roaring traffic on one side and an endless sea of new housing estates on the other. Unfortunately, this makes for a pretty boring day of riding! Triathletes and time trialists flew past me all day while I trundled along on my lumbering steel framed beast. A hot wind blew from the south west ensuring any attempt on my part to hurry things along soon came to a leg aching end. Poor route selection would ensure that I didn't stay bored for long...

I exited the Kwinana Freeway shared path and proceeded down Pinjarra Road in the direction of the town the road was named for. There was a decent sized shoulder to ride in but the speed of the traffic to my right was way too high for comfort. Unfortunately no alternative routes existed that didn't involve many kilometres of backtracking. With the day getting later, I pushed on cursing the drivers who no doubt cursed me too. It wasn't fun at the time, but it provided a good, stern lesson in the importance of planning a decent route. Google maps is not to be relied on in these matters!

I arrived in Pinjarra keen for a break. Subway, a bit of chocolate for dessert and water were acquired here. As is the case with most camp sites in Western Australia, my intended destination did not have running potable water so I filled my bladder (reusable goon bag) with about 4-5 litres to last me an evening and get me going in the morning too. A fair weight penalty, but it beats dehydration.

The traffic on the road from Pinjarra to Dwellingup was far less intense, however the wind did not let up. The foliage in the rural fields around me had literally grown used to the area's prevailing winds, grass and trees alike bent and pointing in the direction I was coming from. I'm not one of those erudite types who can drop a relevant quote into conversation but there is one that came vaguely to mind as I pedalled into the hot breeze that afternoon. Take it away Google:

"It is by riding a bicycle that you learn the contours of a country best, since you have to sweat up the hills and coast down them. Thus you remember them as they actually are, while in a motor car only a high hill impresses you, and you have no such accurate remembrance of country you have driven through as you gain by riding a bicycle." ~ Ernest Hemingway

I'm writing this months after the ride itself yet I remember the landscape well. I hope I will have similar recollections of my trip in the USA. I'm not there for long, but the slow pace of my bicycle will hopefully reveal something about each town, county and state I ride through that might otherwise be missed from the driver's seat of a car. It seems hard to imagine I'll easily forget the name of a mountain pass if I spend a whole morning of my life fighting up it. I guess we'll see.

The town of Dwellingup dwells up in the Darling Ranges, a long steep climb up from the farmlands below. Encumbered by my extra water and with legs that could honestly have used a bit more muscle, I jumped off the bike for a breather twice on the way up. A quick stop in town for a Dr Pepper saw me the last 8km to the National Park entrance. Here I discovered that my allotted site was another 9km down a bone-shaking unsealed road. If you think corrugated road is bad in a car, try it on a steel framed touring bike with no suspension and let me know how you get on!

My camp that night was interesting. People really don't spare any expense when it comes to camping these days. I had a little corner to myself but nearby, my neighbours had occupied multiple sites and set themselves up with a home away from home. Can you really say you went camping if you brought a satellite dish, a generator and a TV?

As the sun went down, the volume increased on what I can honestly say was the shittest music I've ever heard. It wasn't quite country and it wasn't quite rock'n'roll. It was just "blergh". The owner of the music must have been certain that everyone in camp that night was into it because it was LOUD. I laid on my mattress and tried to read a book with my earphones in but there was no stopping the onslaught.

Disgusted, I grabbed my boots and went for a walk down the corrugated road to see what I could see. As I trekked further away from camp, the din was muffled by the dense forest and eventually faded away into nothingness. Peace at last! Kookaburras laughed as the last light of day faded away. Then I heard the thud of what I assume was a mob of roos bouncing around in the bush. I shone my torch into the darkness and saw the reflection of two bright eyes looking back at me in the road. To me, these are the moments you hope for when you go camping in Australia. Feeling satisfied, I returned to camp where the music continued as loud as ever, but by this point I was tired enough to sleep through it. Out like a light.

I awoke at about 5am the next morning to a silent camp site. Donning my bathers, I headed down to the nearby river for a quick swim. The water was beautiful and I enjoyed the feeling of salt and grime washing away from my skin. A great way to start the day!

My ride home was relatively uneventful. I was concerned that I might be a bit wrecked after the previous day's long ride however a good night's sleep did wonders. I ran into a couple of Mandurah locals out for a training ride on the Kwinana Freeway shared path. It was suggested that I install aero bars on my touring bike for another position to sit in on the long rides. It's not a setup I've seen thus far in all my bicycle touring research. I tried to explain a few reasons why this might not be ideal but was given the further advice that a set of carbon fibre wheels could also lower my bike weight and improve my average speed. Not sure if I was being trolled at this point, but the advice seemed earnest. I recounted my tale of woe on Pinjarra Road and the gents provided me with an alternative route for next time. Can confirm, is much better route. Oh well!

I arrived back home just after midday with the mercury approaching 40C. Spring in the US will be juuust a bit colder.
