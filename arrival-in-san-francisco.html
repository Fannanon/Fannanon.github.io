Arrival in San Francisco

Wednesday, 30th March 2016

So the bike arrived safe and sound! It had to with all the bubble wrap we used. I'll need to refuse a lot of polystyrene bags to make up for that egregious use of plastic.

My flight could have been better but I managed to pass the time watching, among other things, a documentary about the Race Across America. It followed the Veloroos, a team of steely lasses from Australia who crossed the U.S on their bikes in less than 7 days. True grit, though our tours have little in common other than being on two wheels!

The San Francisco International bike assembly station is a great idea. It's nothing fancy mind you, just a Park Tools bike stand out in the court yard where busses collect and deposit passengers. Be warned - if you intend to fly into SFO and bring your bike in a cardboard box as I did, there isn't really anywhere appropriate to dispose of it once you're done! I asked the cleaner doing the rounds if it was okay to use the nearby rubbish bins and he didn't seem to mind too much, though I can't be sure as I don't think he spoke much (any) english. I shredded and compacted the heavy box and squished it in as best I could!! Assembly of a bike after a long flight is also a trial. I copped a few strange looks as I swore and cursed returning the bike to a usable state.

I think I'll get a professional to give the bike a proper service before I hit the road in earnest. A ride through the streets of San Francisco revealed some sloppy gear changes and a few strange vibrations that just don't feel quite right. It's a great city to ride in though! Wide streets and drivers who are more than used to bikes on the road.

I also ran into a fellow bike tourist, a guy by the name of Frank who was on the way to his local bike shop to have the oil changed on his fancy folding bike. We exchanged pleasantries and he showed me a picture of his awesome folding bike and trailer setup that following my SFO ordeal, had me questioning whether a full size bike was really where it's at for touring. It was great to meet someone so friendly mere hours into my time in the city, so cheers to you Frank!

Not sure how I've managed to stay awake at this point! Think I could use some bed. Tomorrow is my first real day in the U.S!,
